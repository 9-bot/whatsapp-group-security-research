Making a corporate WhatsApp usage policy executable: controls, evidence and review

A usage policy that lives only as a PDF is a rule nobody deployed. The document can name roles, posting hours, a link policy and an offboarding duty, and the rooms can still be configured by whoever opened them. This note treats the policy as something that has to compile: each clause should point to a control, a place where evidence lands, and a review date.

The clauses themselves, and a template you can copy, are on the official 9bot page about a corporate WhatsApp usage policy. 9bot is commercial software. Compile each domain, or admit that it is theatre

The guide groups the policy into five domains. An executable version asks one extra question in each.

Roles. The document should name duties (owner, administrator, moderator, reviewer), not people. The compiled form is a register of rooms plus two company-controlled admins in every room, one of them the operations number the bot uses. If a room was opened from a personal phone, record the hard limit discussed in the portfolio review DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/G6HF9: whoever opened a standard group in the first place cannot be pushed out. Plan a successor room rather than a removal you cannot perform.

Content and hours. Purpose in the description, posting windows, approved domains, banned language, no unauthorised promotion. The compiled form is a link filter with a domain allowlist, banned words, antispam and antiflood, and scheduled posting so that company messages do not leak into the night. A clause without a matching switch will be enforced only when someone is looking.

Personal data. Phone number, display name and photo are personal data under rules such as the LGPD and the GDPR. The compiled form answers four operational questions: the purpose of the room, who is allowed to extract a roster or a transcript, and where that export is stored, how quickly you discard records of people who already exited, and how someone requests erasure or departure. "We do not export" is an answer. "Anyone with a phone can forward a list" is also an answer, just not one compliance will like.

Moderation. The compiled form is the warning ladder and the logs. 9bot ships a default ladder (observed in the dashboard on 28 Oct 2026): a couple of notices, a last warning, then a removal. Each company can reshape those steps. Evidence lives in the Moderation area, in the deletion log itself, and in the reporting view for moderation, which summarises spam and URLs that were stopped, plus warnings and sanctions in the period you choose.

Offboarding. The compiled form is a same-day job: on the last calendar day you drop that person's admin role and their panel login, you make sure two company admins are still present, and you review linked devices on company numbers. Admin rights ride on phone accounts, so they outlive an HR ticket unless a person revokes them.

Evidence is part of the build

After an incident, someone will want a reconstruction: which item was taken down, at what time, and by what clause. A folder of screenshots in a personal gallery is not an answer you can hand over. Design the policy so that the logs you already have are the answer, and say in the document which screen is authoritative. If a control does not leave a record, either accept that gap in writing or do not claim the control exists.

Review is a cycle, not a launch

A policy change is a change to a live room. Rehearse a new banned word or a new domain rule in a test group before you roll it out to customers. Put a review interval in the document and a team that owns it. A weekly look can confirm that the inventory still shows two company-controlled admins in each room, that the allowlist for links is active, and that the stated review interval has not already lapsed.

This is narrower than a full security programme. Two-step verification on admin accounts still matters, for reasons the takeover review DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/HZUN5 makes clear, and it belongs in the identity process. The executable policy only claims what the rooms themselves can apply and what the logs can show. 9bot is the execution layer for those controls. The register, the review calendar and the offboarding checklist stay with the company.
